Sahara Desert with Kids: A Family Guide to Morocco's Dunes

Ask us about your family's trip

Yes, the Sahara works with children, and for many families it is the best memory of Morocco. What makes or breaks it is not the desert. It is the drive, the bedtime and the cold night. This guide shows you how to get those three right for your child's age.

The short answer

  • Under 3: stay close. A night in the Agafay desert is 40 to 60 minutes from Marrakech.
  • 3 to 5: Merzouga is fine on a private tour of 4 days or more, with no drive over about 6 hours.
  • 6 to 11: the sweet spot. Camels, sandboarding, a family buggy and a night in the dunes.
  • Teens: add quads, a longer camel trek or the wilder Erg Chigaga dunes.
  • Best months: March to May and September to November. Avoid June to August with young children.

What parents worry about, and the fix

  • "The drive is too long for them."

    Take 4 days or more, so no day is over about 6 hours, or stay close in Agafay. On a private tour you stop for every toilet break and playground.

  • "Dinner at camp is too late."

    Camp dinners are usually around 8 pm. We ask the camp for an early dinner for the children.

  • "They will be cold at night."

    From November to March nights get close to freezing. Choose a camp with heating in winter, and pack a warm layer and a hat.

  • "What about the toilets?"

    Book a tent with its own bathroom and hot shower, so there is no walk in the dark. See luxury vs standard camps.

  • "My child is scared of camels."

    Part of the family can ride to the camp by 4×4 and meet the others there. Nobody misses the sunset.

  • "The sun is too strong."

    The UV index in the south often passes 12. Do the dunes at sunset and sunrise, and rest at midday.

  • "What if someone gets sick?"

    Your driver and car stay with you the whole trip. Merzouga has no hospital, but the towns of Rissani and Erfoud are close. Bring a family medical kit.

  • "Do you have car seats?"

    Yes, child and baby seats on request. Tell us the ages when you book.

Sahara with Kids FAQ

What is the best age to take kids to the Sahara?

From about 6, children enjoy everything: their own camel, sandboarding and a buggy. Younger children can go too, on a slower private tour of 4 days or more, or to Agafay near Marrakech.

Can we take a baby or toddler to the desert?

Yes. For babies and toddlers, a night in Agafay, 40 to 60 minutes from Marrakech, is the easy choice. Merzouga works on a private tour of 5 days or more, with child seats and stops whenever you need them.

How old do children need to be to ride a camel?

From about 3, a child shares a camel with a parent, and the camel man leads it on foot. From about 6, most children ride their own camel, still led by the guide.

Is the 3-day desert tour too much for children?

Often, yes. Its last day is 9 to 10 hours in the car back to Marrakech. With young children, take 4 days or more so no day is over about 6 hours.

Is the Sahara safe for kids?

Yes, with the right trip. The roads to Merzouga are paved, the camels walk slowly and are led by hand, and your driver stays with you all trip. The real risks are sun, heat and cold nights, so pick the right months and pack well.

When is the best time to visit the Sahara with kids?

March to May and September to November. Winter days are sunny but nights get close to freezing; from June to August the dunes pass 40 °C, too hot for young children.
