The Best Morocco Itinerary for First-Time Visitors

4 January 2026 · Morocco Tours Hub

Planning your first trip to Morocco and not sure how many days you need or where to go? The right itinerary depends on how much time you have and what you want to see. This guide breaks down the best Morocco itineraries for first-time visitors, so you can choose with confidence.

How Many Days Do You Need in Morocco?

A week is enough for a first taste — Marrakech, the Sahara and Fes. Ten days lets you add Chefchaouen and the coast, and two weeks covers the whole country from the Mediterranean to the dunes. If you only have a few days, focus on Marrakech plus a desert trip rather than trying to see everything.

Best Morocco Itineraries by Length

5 Days: Marrakech, the Sahara & Fes

The shortest route that still includes an overnight in the desert. Cross the High Atlas, sleep in a Sahara camp at Merzouga, and finish in Fes. See our Morocco in 5 Days itinerary for the day-by-day plan.

7 Days: The Classic First-Timer Route

One week is the sweet spot — two nights in Marrakech and Fes bookend the desert crossing. It's the most popular first-time route. See The Ultimate 7-Day Morocco Itinerary.

8–10 Days: Add Chefchaouen & the Coast

With eight to ten days you can add a full desert day, the blue city of Chefchaouen and the Atlantic cities. See our 8-Day and 10-Day itineraries.

12–16 Days: See the Whole Country

Two weeks or more lets you combine the north, the imperial cities, the Sahara, Marrakech and the coast at a relaxed pace. See our 12-Day, 14-Day and 16-Day itineraries.

Where Should You Start?

Marrakech and Casablanca have the largest international airports and are the most common starting points. Fes is ideal if you want to begin with the desert, and Tangier suits travellers arriving from Spain. Flying into one city and out of another (open-jaw) lets you see more without backtracking.

The Must-Dos on a First Trip

For most first-timers the essentials are Marrakech's medina and souks, a night in the Sahara at Merzouga, the kasbah of Aït Ben Haddou, and the medieval city of Fes. Add Chefchaouen and Essaouira if time allows.

Best Time for a First Trip

Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) offer the most comfortable weather across all regions. Summer is hot inland and in the desert; winter is mild by day but cold in the desert and mountains at night.

Private vs Group Tours

A private tour offers the most flexibility over pace and stops and is ideal for couples, families and small groups. Shared group tours are a cheaper option focused mainly on the desert. See our desert tour guide for a full comparison.

Final Thoughts

There's no single 'best' Morocco itinerary — the right one matches your time and interests. For most first-timers, 7 to 10 days combining Marrakech, the Sahara and Fes is the ideal introduction, with room to return for more.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days do you need in Morocco for a first visit?

Seven to ten days is ideal for a first trip, enough to combine Marrakech, the Sahara and Fes without rushing. Five days works for a focused taste; two weeks covers the whole country.

What is the best first-time Morocco itinerary?

The classic 7-day Marrakech–Sahara–Fes route is the most popular for first-timers. Add Chefchaouen and the coast with 10+ days.

Where should I fly into for Morocco?

Marrakech and Casablanca have the biggest airports. Flying into one and out of another avoids backtracking.

Is Morocco easy to travel for first-timers?

Yes, especially on a private tour with a driver-guide, which removes the stress of long drives and navigation and lets you focus on the experience.

What should I not miss in Morocco?

Marrakech's medina, a night in the Merzouga Sahara, Aït Ben Haddou and the city of Fes are the top highlights for a first visit.
