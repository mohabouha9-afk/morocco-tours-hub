Morocco Desert Tours: The Complete Guide for First-Time Visitors

9 November 2025 · Morocco Tours Hub

If you are planning your first trip to Morocco, a Morocco desert tour is one of the experiences you should seriously consider adding to your itinerary. From the dramatic High Atlas Mountains and ancient kasbahs to the golden dunes of the Sahara, the journey to the desert is about much more than simply spending one night in a camp.

For most first-time visitors, Merzouga and the Erg Chebbi dunes offer the classic Moroccan Sahara experience. Here you can ride a camel across golden sand dunes at sunset, spend the night in a desert camp, enjoy traditional Moroccan food and music, and wake up early to watch the sunrise over the Sahara. Erg Chebbi is also one of Morocco's most accessible major dune areas, making it particularly suitable for travelers combining the desert with Marrakech, Fes or a longer Morocco itinerary.

But choosing the right desert tour requires some planning. How many days should you spend? Should you travel from Marrakech or Fes? Is Merzouga better than Zagora? Should you choose a private or shared tour? What is a desert camp really like? And what should you pack?

This guide answers the most important questions before booking your Morocco Sahara desert tour.

What Is a Morocco Desert Tour?

A Morocco desert tour is usually a multi-day journey connecting one of Morocco's major cities with the country's desert regions. The most popular routes include:

  • Marrakech to Merzouga
  • Marrakech to Fes via Merzouga
  • Fes to Merzouga
  • Fes to Marrakech via Merzouga
  • Marrakech to Zagora
  • Marrakech to M'Hamid and Erg Chigaga
  • Longer private Morocco tours combining the Sahara with Fes, Marrakech, Casablanca, Chefchaouen and the Atlantic coast

A desert tour is not simply transportation to the dunes. The route itself is part of the experience. Depending on your itinerary, you may cross the High Atlas Mountains, visit Aït Ben Haddou, explore Ouarzazate, walk through the Dades Valley and Todra Gorge, visit traditional villages and oases, and finally reach the Sahara.

That is why we recommend choosing an itinerary that gives you enough time rather than trying to reach the desert as quickly as possible.

Which Desert Should You Visit in Morocco?

One of the first decisions you need to make is which desert experience is right for you. Morocco has several desert regions, and they are not all the same.

Merzouga and Erg Chebbi

For most first-time visitors, Merzouga is the best choice. Merzouga is located beside the spectacular Erg Chebbi dunes in southeastern Morocco. The area is famous for its large golden sand dunes, camel trekking, desert camps and spectacular sunrises and sunsets. Some dunes reach around 150 metres.

Merzouga is particularly good for travelers who want the classic Sahara experience seen in photographs:

  • Golden sand dunes
  • Camel trekking
  • Desert camps
  • Sahara sunsets
  • Sahara sunrises
  • Sandboarding
  • 4×4 desert excursions
  • Nomad visits
  • Gnawa music in Khamlia
  • Stargazing

For a first Morocco desert trip, Erg Chebbi is usually our recommended option.

Zagora

Zagora is closer to Marrakech and is therefore suitable for travelers with very limited time. A two-day Marrakech–Zagora trip can include the High Atlas Mountains, Aït Ben Haddou, Ouarzazate, the Draa Valley, a camel ride and a desert camp.

However, travelers should understand that Zagora does not offer the same large golden dune landscape as Merzouga. It is a practical choice when time is limited, while Merzouga is generally the better option for travelers specifically looking for the iconic Sahara dunes.

Choose Zagora if: you only have two days.
Choose Merzouga if: you want the classic golden-dune Sahara experience.

Erg Chigaga

For travelers looking for a more remote desert experience, Erg Chigaga, near M'Hamid, is another option. It is more remote and requires additional travel, including a 4×4 section to reach the dunes. This makes it better suited to travelers who have more time and want a quieter, more adventurous desert experience.

How Many Days Do You Need for a Morocco Desert Tour?

This is one of the most important questions to ask before booking.

2 Days

A 2-day Marrakech to Zagora tour is possible and is best for travelers with very limited time. However, we would not recommend trying to visit Merzouga from Marrakech in only two days. The distances are long, and you would spend too much of the trip driving.

3 Days

A 3-day Marrakech to Merzouga tour is one of the most popular choices. A typical itinerary is:

Day 1: Marrakech → High Atlas → Aït Ben Haddou → Ouarzazate → Dades Valley
Day 2: Dades → Todra Gorge → Erfoud → Rissani → Merzouga → camel trek → desert camp
Day 3: Sahara sunrise → return toward Marrakech or continue to Fes

The road journey from Marrakech to Merzouga is long, so splitting the route over several days makes the experience much more enjoyable. Current travel guides generally recommend at least three days for the Marrakech–Merzouga route.

4 Days or More

If you have more time, four or five days can provide a much more relaxed experience. You can spend more time in the Dades Valley, Todra Gorge and Merzouga instead of treating them simply as stops along the road.

For travelers who want a more comfortable experience, we often recommend two nights in the Merzouga area. This gives you a full day for a 4×4 excursion, visiting nomads, Khamlia and other desert attractions without immediately leaving the Sahara.

Morocco Desert Tours from Marrakech

Marrakech is one of the most popular starting points for Morocco desert tours. A typical Marrakech to Merzouga route passes through:

Marrakech → High Atlas Mountains → Aït Ben Haddou → Ouarzazate → Dades Valley → Todra Gorge → Erfoud → Rissani → Merzouga

The route is scenic and varied. You begin in the busy Red City, cross the Atlas Mountains and gradually enter southern Morocco. The landscape changes from mountains and valleys to palm oases and finally the desert.

For first-time visitors, a 3-day or 4-day Marrakech to Merzouga tour is usually much better than trying to make the journey in a very short time.

Morocco Desert Tours from Fes

Fes is another excellent starting point for a Sahara adventure. The route from Fes is different from the Marrakech route. You can travel through:

Fes → Ifrane → Azrou → Middle Atlas → Midelt → Ziz Valley → Erfoud → Merzouga

One of the highlights is the transition from the green landscapes and cedar forests of the Middle Atlas to the dry landscapes of southeastern Morocco. Travelers can stop around Ifrane and Azrou, continue through the Middle Atlas Mountains and then descend toward the Ziz Valley before reaching Merzouga.

A Fes-to-Merzouga journey can also be combined with a longer route ending in Marrakech.

What Is a Sahara Desert Camp Like?

A desert camp can range from a simple traditional bivouac to a comfortable luxury camp.

Standard Desert Camps

Standard camps are designed for travelers who want the essential Sahara experience at a lower price. Depending on the camp, facilities may be shared and accommodation is generally simpler.

Comfort Camps

Comfort camps usually offer better beds, private facilities and additional amenities. They are a good option for travelers who want comfort without choosing the highest luxury category.

Luxury Desert Camps

Luxury camps can include:

  • Private tents
  • Private bathrooms
  • Comfortable beds
  • Electricity
  • Heating or air conditioning depending on the camp
  • Private dining
  • Higher-quality furnishings

If comfort is important to you, check exactly what is included rather than choosing a camp simply because the word "luxury" appears in its name. Camp standards vary, so it is important to ask about bathrooms, heating, air conditioning, electricity and the exact location of the camp before booking.

What Happens During a Sahara Desert Experience?

A typical Merzouga desert experience begins in the afternoon. After arriving in Merzouga, you leave your vehicle and meet the camel guides. You then ride through the dunes toward the desert camp. The camel ride usually takes around one to two hours, depending on the route and camp location.

The highlight is reaching a high dune as the sun begins to disappear. After sunset, continue to the camp and enjoy a traditional Moroccan dinner. The evening may include:

  • Berber music
  • Traditional drums
  • Campfire
  • Tea
  • Stargazing
  • Time to relax in the dunes

Because there is very little artificial light away from the towns, the night sky can be spectacular. The following morning, wake early for sunrise before returning to Merzouga.

What Can You Do in Merzouga?

Merzouga is more than a camel ride. If you spend two nights in the area, you can explore the desert and surrounding villages by 4×4. Popular activities include:

Camel Trekking

The classic Sahara experience. Ride through the dunes around sunset and return after sunrise or by 4×4.

Sandboarding

Sandboarding is a fun activity for both adults and older children. Many camps provide boards.

4×4 Desert Excursion

A 4×4 excursion allows you to explore areas that are difficult to reach on foot or by camel.

Visit a Nomad Family

A visit to a nomadic family can provide insight into traditional desert life and how families adapt to the harsh environment.

Khamlia Village

Khamlia is known for Gnawa music, an important musical tradition in southeastern Morocco.

Rissani

Rissani is an important historic market town and can be particularly interesting on market days.

Desert Sunrise and Sunset

You do not need an expensive activity to enjoy the Sahara. Simply climbing a dune and watching the changing colors of the sand can be one of the best moments of the trip.

What Is the Best Time for a Morocco Desert Tour?

The Sahara can be visited throughout the year, but temperatures vary considerably.

Spring: March to May

Spring is generally an excellent season for desert travel. Days are warm without the extreme heat associated with summer, while evenings are more comfortable.

Summer: June to August

Summer can be extremely hot in the desert. If you travel during these months, activities are better planned around the early morning and late afternoon. Travelers should be prepared for very high daytime temperatures.

Autumn: September to November

Autumn is another excellent period for a Sahara tour. Temperatures become more comfortable and the desert is particularly pleasant for camel trekking and outdoor activities.

Winter: December to February

Winter days can be comfortable, but desert nights can become very cold. If you are traveling in December or January, bring warm layers, especially for the evening and early morning.

What Should You Pack for the Sahara?

You do not need a huge suitcase for a desert tour. We recommend bringing:

  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Lightweight daytime clothing
  • Warm layers for the evening
  • A warm jacket in winter
  • Hat or cap
  • Sunglasses
  • Sunscreen
  • Scarf or shawl
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Small backpack
  • Phone/camera
  • Power bank
  • Headlamp or small flashlight
  • Personal medication

A scarf is particularly useful because it protects you from sun and wind and is also practical when walking through the dunes. Remember that desert temperatures can change significantly between daytime and nighttime.

Private vs Shared Morocco Desert Tours

Another important decision is whether to choose a private or shared desert tour.

Shared Group Tour

A shared tour is generally cheaper because transportation and some services are shared with other travelers. It can be a good choice for:

  • Solo travelers
  • Students
  • Young travelers
  • Budget travelers
  • People who enjoy meeting other tourists

The disadvantage is less flexibility. The itinerary and departure times are generally fixed.

Private Desert Tour

A private tour gives you more flexibility. You travel with your own vehicle and driver, and the itinerary can be adapted to your interests. Private tours are particularly suitable for:

  • Couples
  • Families
  • Friends
  • Honeymooners
  • Small groups
  • Travelers who want more comfort

You can spend longer at places you enjoy and make additional stops for photographs or local experiences. For travelers who have the budget, we generally recommend a private tour because the journey to the Sahara is long and flexibility makes a noticeable difference.

Is a Morocco Desert Tour Suitable for Families?

Yes. A Sahara tour can be a fantastic family experience, especially when the itinerary is planned at a comfortable pace. Families should consider:

  • The age of children
  • Driving distances
  • Desert temperatures
  • Accommodation standards
  • Bathroom facilities
  • Camel trekking duration

For families with young children, a private tour is often the most comfortable option because the itinerary can be adjusted around the family's needs.

Is a Morocco Desert Tour Suitable for Older Travelers?

Yes, but the itinerary should be planned carefully. You do not have to spend a long time riding a camel. If necessary, the camel experience can be shortened or replaced with a 4×4 transfer. A private tour also allows more frequent breaks and a slower pace. The important thing is to communicate your needs before booking.

How Much Does a Morocco Desert Tour Cost?

There is no single price for a Morocco desert tour. The cost depends on:

  • Number of travelers
  • Number of days
  • Private or shared transportation
  • Hotel category
  • Desert camp category
  • Season
  • Included activities
  • Local guides
  • Route
  • Number of nights in Merzouga

A budget shared tour will cost considerably less than a private luxury itinerary. Instead of choosing a tour only because it has the lowest price, compare what is actually included. Ask:

  • Is transportation included?
  • What type of accommodation is included?
  • Is the desert camp inside the dunes?
  • Are meals included?
  • Is the camel trek included?
  • Is the tour private or shared?
  • What happens if the itinerary needs to change?

These questions can help you understand the real value of the tour.

What to Look for When Booking a Morocco Desert Tour

Before making your reservation, check the following:

1. Read the Complete Itinerary

Make sure you understand where you will sleep each night and how much driving is involved.

2. Check the Accommodation

Do not assume every desert camp is the same.

3. Ask About the Vehicle

For private tours, ask what type of vehicle will be used and how many passengers it accommodates.

4. Check What Is Included

A low advertised price can become much more expensive when activities, meals and transfers are added separately.

5. Look at Recent Reviews

Recent traveler experiences can help you understand the quality of the operator and accommodation.

6. Ask About the Finish City

Some tours return to Marrakech, while others continue to Fes or another city. A Marrakech to Fes via Merzouga tour can be a smart option because you experience the Sahara without returning to Marrakech.

How to Choose the Right Morocco Desert Tour

For a first-time visitor, our general recommendations are:

  • Only 2 days: Choose Zagora.
  • 3 days: Choose Marrakech to Merzouga.
  • 4 days: Choose a more relaxed Marrakech to Merzouga itinerary.
  • 5+ days: Consider spending additional time in Merzouga and southern Morocco.
  • Traveling from Fes: Consider Fes → Merzouga → Marrakech.
  • Want maximum flexibility: Choose a private tour.
  • Traveling on a limited budget: Consider a shared group tour.
  • Want more comfort: Choose superior hotels and a private desert camp.

Our Recommended Morocco Desert Experience

If this is your first trip to Morocco and you have enough time, we recommend choosing a 3- or 4-day Marrakech to Merzouga desert tour rather than trying to rush through the route. The journey can include:

Marrakech → High Atlas Mountains → Aït Ben Haddou → Ouarzazate → Dades Valley → Todra Gorge → Erfoud → Rissani → Merzouga

Then enjoy: Camel trek → Sahara sunset → desert camp → dinner → Berber music → sunrise.

If you have more time, add a second night in Merzouga. This allows you to explore the surrounding desert by 4×4, visit a nomadic family, discover Khamlia and enjoy the Sahara without rushing.

Final Thoughts: Is a Morocco Desert Tour Worth It?

For most first-time visitors, yes. The Sahara is not simply a place you visit and photograph. The road to the desert introduces you to a completely different side of Morocco: mountain villages, ancient kasbahs, palm-filled valleys, traditional communities and vast landscapes.

The key is choosing the right itinerary. If you have only two days, consider Zagora. If you want the classic golden dunes, choose Merzouga and Erg Chebbi. If you can spare three or four days, slow the journey down and enjoy the places between Marrakech and the Sahara.

And if you want the most flexibility, a private Morocco desert tour allows you to travel at your own pace, choose your accommodation level and spend more time at the places that interest you most.

The Sahara is a long way from Marrakech — but the journey is part of the experience.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best desert in Morocco for first-time visitors?

For most first-time visitors, Merzouga and Erg Chebbi are the best choice because of the dramatic golden dunes and established range of desert experiences.

How many days do I need for a Morocco desert tour?

For Marrakech to Merzouga, we recommend at least 3 days and 2 nights. Four days or more allows for a more relaxed experience.

Can I visit the Sahara from Marrakech in 2 days?

Yes, but we recommend Zagora for a two-day Marrakech desert trip. Merzouga is much farther away and is better suited to a 3-day or longer itinerary.

Is Merzouga worth visiting?

Yes. Merzouga provides the classic golden-dune Sahara experience, including camel trekking, desert camps, sunsets and sunrises.

Should I choose a private or shared Morocco desert tour?

A shared tour is generally better for travelers focused on budget, while a private tour provides greater flexibility and comfort.

What is the best month for a Sahara tour?

Spring and autumn are particularly comfortable. Winter can also be excellent during the day, although desert nights can be very cold. Summer is much hotter and requires more careful planning.

Can I go from Marrakech to Fes through the Sahara?

Yes. A Marrakech to Fes via Merzouga tour is an excellent way to experience the Sahara without returning to Marrakech.

Is camel trekking difficult?

The camel ride is generally manageable for most travelers, but sitting on a camel for an extended period can be uncomfortable. Shorter rides or 4×4 alternatives can be arranged.

Do I need a 4×4 to reach Merzouga?

No. The road to Merzouga is paved. A 4×4 is useful for exploring the dunes and surrounding desert areas rather than simply reaching the village.

What should I wear in the Sahara?

Wear comfortable clothing, closed shoes, sun protection and layers. Bring a warm jacket for winter nights and early mornings.
