From Fes to the Sahara

Fes is the closest imperial city to Merzouga. The road climbs into the cedar forests of the Middle Atlas, where Barbary macaques live by the roadside, crosses the high plateau to Midelt, then drops through the palm gorges of the Ziz Valley to the Erg Chebbi dunes. It is about 470 km, a full day with stops, and you reach the camp in time for the sunset.

Choose a round trip if you fly home from Fes, or a one-way tour if you are heading on to Marrakech: it replaces the seven-hour train and shows you the whole south on the way. Every tour below is private, with riad pick-up and the same driver-guide all the way.

Desert Tours from Fes FAQ

How far is Merzouga from Fes?

About 470 km, eight hours with stops for coffee in Ifrane, the cedar forest and lunch in Midelt. The road crosses the Middle Atlas, then follows the palm gorges of the Ziz Valley down to the dunes, and you arrive in time for the sunset camel ride.

Is a 2-day desert tour from Fes enough?

For one night in the dunes, yes: the sunset camel ride, dinner at the camp and the sunrise. But each day is about eight hours on the road. The 3-day tour adds a full day around Merzouga, so you don’t spend two days in the car for one night in the dunes.

Can you pick us up at a riad in the Fes medina?

Yes. Cars can’t enter the medina, so your driver meets you at the nearest gate, often with someone from your riad to help with the bags. Send us your riad’s name and we confirm the meeting point and time on WhatsApp.

Should we come back to Fes or end in Marrakech?

If you fly home from Fes, take a round trip. If your trip goes on to Marrakech, end there: the train takes about seven hours by Rabat and Casablanca and sees none of the south, while our tours make the same move by the Sahara, the gorges and Aït Ben Haddou.

Can we feed the macaques in the cedar forest?

Please don’t. Sellers by the road offer nuts and bread for the monkeys, but feeding makes them sick and aggressive. Your driver stops where you can watch them quietly from close by.

Where do we eat lunch on the way?

Wherever you like. Your driver suggests a few local places in Midelt and you choose; there is no fixed restaurant and no buffet we take you to. Lunch is usually about €10–15 each.
