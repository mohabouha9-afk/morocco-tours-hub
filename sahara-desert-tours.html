A Night in the Sahara, Your Way

The Sahara is the night most travellers remember from Morocco: a camel ride as the dunes turn red, dinner by the fire, and more stars than you have ever seen. We run every desert tour privately, with one driver-guide from the first morning to the last, and no shopping stops on the way.

Choosing comes down to three things: which desert, how many days, and where you want to end. Merzouga has the tallest dunes but sits 9 to 10 hours from Marrakech, so we split the drive over two or three days. Zagora is closer, with lower dunes, and Erg Chigaga is the wild one, reached by 4×4. Pick your starting city below and see every tour that leaves from it, round trip or one way.

Desert Tour FAQ

Which desert should we choose: Merzouga, Zagora, Erg Chigaga or Agafay?

For tall golden dunes, choose Merzouga: Erg Chebbi rises to about 150 m, and 3 days from Marrakech is the best length. Zagora is closer, with lower dunes, and fits 2 days. Erg Chigaga is wild and empty, reached by 4×4, in 3 days or more. Agafay is a stony desert 40 minutes from Marrakech, made for an evening.

How many days do we need for a Sahara tour?

Two days is possible, but Merzouga is 9 to 10 hours from Marrakech each way, so it is mostly driving. Three days is the classic, with a night in the Dades Valley and a night in the dunes. Four days or more gives you a full day in Merzouga and no long drive day.

Are your desert tours private? Can I come alone?

Yes, every desert tour on this page is private: your own vehicle and driver-guide, and your own timings. Solo travellers can book too. If you would rather share the cost with other travellers, ask about our 3-day shared group tour from Marrakech.

When is the best time for a desert tour?

March to May and September to November, with warm days and cool nights. From June to August the dunes pass 40 °C, so we ride at sunset and sunrise. Winter days are sunny, but nights drop close to freezing: bring a warm jacket for the evening and the sunrise.

Is the Sahara safe for tourists?

Yes. The desert routes are busy, well-kept roads with regular police checkpoints, and your driver-guide stays with you for the whole trip. Keep your passport with you, as checkpoints sometimes ask for it.

What is the desert camp like?

You sleep in a tent with a real bed, and in our Merzouga camps every tent has its own bathroom. Dinner, music by the fire and breakfast are included. Each tour page lets you choose a standard, deluxe or luxury camp.
