Overview

Eighteen days down the Atlantic side of Morocco, from the Red City to the turquoise lagoon of Dakhla. Marrakech with a local guide, two slow days in Essaouira, the surf town of Taghazout, then inland to the painted rocks and almond valleys of Tafraoute in the Anti-Atlas.

Back on the coast at Mirleft for the red arch of Legzira and the Spanish streets of Sidi Ifni, then the great road where the Sahara runs into the sea: the Khnifiss lagoon, Saint-Exupéry’s Tarfaya and Laayoune. Five nights in Dakhla to finish, for the White Dune, the oyster farms and the kite lagoon. Your own vehicle and driver-guide all the way.

Itinerary

  1. 1
    Day 1

    Marrakech: arrival

    Your driver-guide meets you at Marrakech airport with your name on a sign, at whatever hour you land, and takes you to your riad. If there is time, a first walk to Jemaa el-Fnaa as the food stalls light up.

    • Airport to your riad, about 20 minutes
    • Night: Marrakech riad
    • Breakfast from day 2
  2. 2
    Day 2

    Marrakech: a guided day in the medina

    With a local Marrakech guide: the Koutoubia, the painted ceilings of the Bahia Palace, the Saadian Tombs, the souks and the Ben Youssef Madrasa. The afternoon is free for the Majorelle Garden, a hammam or a rooftop café.

    • Day on foot in the medina
    • Night: Marrakech riad
    • Breakfast
  3. 3
    Day 3

    Marrakech → Essaouira

    West across the argan plains to the Atlantic. The afternoon in Essaouira’s white and blue medina, on the ramparts of the Skala and at the fishing port with its blue boats, with grilled fish by the harbour if you like.

    • About 180 km, about 3 hours on the road
    • Night: Essaouira
    • Breakfast
  4. 4
    Day 4

    Essaouira: a free day by the sea

    No packing today: the Mellah and the Bayt Dakira house of memory, the woodworkers under the ramparts, a long walk on the beach and the sun setting over the ocean from a rooftop.

    • Free day
    • Night: Essaouira
    • Breakfast
  5. 5
    Day 5

    Essaouira → Imsouane → Taghazout

    South on the coast road through argan hills to Imsouane, the fishing village with one of Africa’s longest waves, for lunch by the bay. On to Taghazout, the surf town, for the sunset.

    • About 170 km, about 3 hours on the road
    • Night: Taghazout
    • Breakfast
  6. 6
    Day 6

    Taghazout: surf, swim or rest

    A day in the surf town at your pace: a surf lesson for beginners in the bay, a walk along the cliffs to the next beach, or the palm-lined rock pools of Paradise Valley in the hills behind, by car.

    • Free day; Paradise Valley on request
    • Night: Taghazout
    • Breakfast
  7. 7
    Day 7

    Taghazout → Agadir → Tafraoute

    The view over the bay from the Agadir Oufella kasbah, then inland over the Anti-Atlas by Aït Baha, through argan and almond country, to Tafraoute in its valley of pink granite boulders.

    • About 185 km, about 3.5 hours on the road
    • Night: Tafraoute
    • Breakfast
  8. 8
    Day 8

    Tafraoute: painted rocks and almond valleys

    A day in the Anti-Atlas: the painted blue boulders in the desert outside town, the villages of the Ammeln Valley under the Jbel Lekst, and the Aït Mansour gorge, a ribbon of palm trees between red cliffs.

    • Short drives around Tafraoute
    • Night: Tafraoute
    • Breakfast
  9. 9
    Day 9

    Tafraoute → Tiznit → Mirleft

    Down from the mountains to Tiznit, the walled town of silversmiths, for its jewellery souk, then to the coast at Mirleft, a quiet village of cliffs and coves, for the evening.

    • About 150 km, about 3 hours on the road
    • Night: Mirleft
    • Breakfast
  10. 10
    Day 10

    Legzira beach and Sidi Ifni

    Low tide on the red sand of Legzira, under its cliffs and its great stone arch, then Sidi Ifni, the old Spanish town with its art deco squares above the ocean. Back in Mirleft for sunset.

    • About 70 km in the day
    • Night: Mirleft
    • Breakfast
  11. 11
    Day 11

    Mirleft → Guelmim → Tan-Tan → Khnifiss lagoon

    Through Guelmim, the old gateway to the Sahara, and Tan-Tan, the land of the Sahrawi nomads, onto the long coast road where the desert meets the ocean. A night at Akhfennir, beside the Khnifiss lagoon and its birds.

    • About 370 km, about 5 hours on the road
    • Night: Akhfennir
    • Breakfast
  12. 12
    Day 12

    Akhfennir → Tarfaya → Laayoune

    Tarfaya, where Saint-Exupéry flew the airmail and dreamed up the Little Prince: the small museum, the old Casa del Mar fort in the waves. Then the dunes to Laayoune, the biggest city of the far south.

    • About 200 km, about 3 hours on the road
    • Night: Laayoune
    • Breakfast
  13. 13
    Day 13

    Laayoune → Boujdour → Dakhla

    The great Atlantic Sahara road, straight and empty between the sand and the sea, with stops whenever you want and lunch in Boujdour. In the late afternoon, the turquoise lagoon of Dakhla opens on your right.

    • About 530 km, about 7 hours on the road
    • Night: Dakhla
    • Breakfast
  14. 14
    Day 14

    Dakhla: the White Dune and the oyster farms

    The lagoon by 4×4 with your driver-guide: the White Dune, which becomes an island at high tide, and an oyster farm on the shore for a tasting straight from the water. Dragon Island when the tide is low.

    • About 80 km around the lagoon
    • Night: Dakhla
    • Breakfast
  15. 15
    Day 15

    Dakhla: on the water

    The lagoon is one of the best kitesurfing spots in the world, with flat water and wind most of the year. Take a first kite or wing lesson, paddle a kayak, or just watch from a beach café.

    • Free day; lessons on request
    • Night: Dakhla
    • Breakfast
  16. 16
    Day 16

    Dakhla: where the desert meets the ocean

    A day along the open coast by 4×4: cliffs of sand above empty beaches, fishermen’s camps and wide views over the Atlantic Sahara, with a picnic lunch by the sea.

    • 4×4 day along the coast
    • Night: Dakhla
    • Breakfast
  17. 17
    Day 17

    Dakhla: a free day

    A last slow day by the lagoon: a swim, a massage, a book in the shade, or one more session on the water before a sunset dinner of fresh fish.

    • Free day
    • Night: Dakhla
    • Breakfast
  18. 18
    Day 18

    Dakhla → the airport

    A free morning, then your driver-guide takes you to Dakhla airport, a few minutes from town, at the time of your flight.

    • Hotel to Dakhla airport, about 15 minutes
    • Ends at Dakhla airport
    • Breakfast

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the road to Dakhla safe?

Yes. The coast road is paved and used every day by locals, trucks and travellers, with police checkpoints where your driver-guide shows your passports. The UK advises against travel only near the Berm, far inland; our route stays on the coast. Check your insurance covers the region.

How long are the drives?

Most days are 3 hours or less. Three are longer: Mirleft to Akhfennir, about 370 km; Akhfennir to Laayoune, about 200 km; and Laayoune to Dakhla, about 530 km and 7 hours on an empty, straight road, always in daylight, with stops whenever you want.

How do we fly home from Dakhla?

Dakhla airport is a few minutes from town. Royal Air Maroc flies to Casablanca for connections, and there are direct flights to Paris-Orly and Madrid. Schedules change with the season, so we check the times with you when you book.

What is there to do for five days in Dakhla?

The White Dune and Dragon Island at low tide, an oyster tasting on the shore, kite, wing or kayak lessons on the flat lagoon, and a 4×4 day along the open coast. Or simply rest: the slow last days are the point.

Do we need to surf to enjoy Taghazout?

No. The bay has gentle waves for a first lesson, but Taghazout is just as good for cliff walks, beach cafés and the rock pools of Paradise Valley. Tell us if you want a lesson and we book it.
