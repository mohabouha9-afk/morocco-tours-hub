Overview

Drive a buggy across the Erg Chebbi dunes with your partner, a friend or your children beside you, following your guide up and over the ridges to the best viewpoints, with stops for photos on top.

Choose one hour in the dunes, or two hours with tea at a nomad family’s tent and the desert lake of Dayet Srij when it holds water. Roll bars, seat belts and helmets keep everyone safe, and family buggies take up to four.

Programme

  1. 1
    Morning or afternoon

    Meet us at the buggy base

    Morning around 9:00, or late afternoon about 2 hours before sunset; we confirm the time on WhatsApp the day before. We meet you at the base by the dunes or at your Merzouga hotel.

  2. 2
    Before the drive

    Helmets and briefing

    Helmets on, seat belts done, then a short briefing and a few slow turns on flat sand until the driver feels at ease.

  3. 3
    On the dunes

    Into Erg Chebbi

    Your guide drives in front on the best lines over the ridges, at a pace that suits you, with stops on top for photos and the view.

  4. 4
    Viewpoint

    High above the dunes

    A stop on a high dune to look out over Erg Chebbi, the palm groves and, on clear days, the Algerian border hills.

  5. 5
    2-hour ride

    Nomad tea and the desert lake

    On the longer ride, mint tea at a nomad family’s tent, then the lake of Dayet Srij, where flamingos sometimes stop after a wet winter.

  6. 6
    After the drive

    Back at the base

    About 1 or 2 hours after you set off, back at the base or your hotel, dusty and smiling.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who can drive the buggy?

Drivers need to be 18 or older with a valid driving licence; bring it with you. No off-road experience is needed: the briefing comes first and your guide sets the pace.

Buggy or quad: which should we choose?

A buggy has seats, seat belts and a roll bar, and you ride side by side, so it suits couples and families. A quad gives everyone their own machine and feels more like riding a bike.

Can children come?

Yes, from 5 years old as passengers, with seat belts and a helmet. With two children, a 4-seat family buggy keeps everyone together.

Is it safe?

Yes, when you follow the guide. He drives in front, picks the lines over the dunes and keeps the speed steady; seat belts and helmets are always worn.

One hour or two?

One hour gives you the dunes and the viewpoints. Two hours goes further, to tea with a nomad family and the desert lake of Dayet Srij when it has water.
