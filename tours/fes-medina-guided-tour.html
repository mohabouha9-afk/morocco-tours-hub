Overview

Fes el-Bali is the oldest and largest medina in Morocco: some 9,000 lanes, no cars, and no map that really works. A local guide turns the maze into a story, from the golden gates of the Royal Palace and the Blue Gate to the carved madrasas, the coppersmiths and the Chouara tannery.

It is your private tour, so you set the pace and choose what to enter. Most of the walk goes downhill, and it ends where you can find your own way back. Do it on your first day in Fes.

Highlights

  • Royal Palace: the seven golden doors on Place des Alaouites
  • Bab Boujeloud: the Blue Gate into the old medina
  • Bou Inania Madrasa: cedar, stucco and zellij from the 14th century
  • Al-Attarine Madrasa: the finest courtyard in Fes, by the Qaraouiyine
  • Seffarine Square: coppersmiths hammering pots in the open
  • Chouara Tannery: the dye pits seen from a terrace above
  • And much more along the way

Programme

  1. 1
    09:00

    Pick-up and the Royal Palace

    Your guide and driver collect you at your riad gate or hotel and drive to the golden doors of the Royal Palace (closed to visitors) for photos. We confirm the time on WhatsApp the day before.

  2. 2
    09:30

    Bab Boujeloud and Bou Inania

    A 10-minute drive to the Blue Gate, then on foot into the Bou Inania Madrasa: carved cedar, stucco and a marble courtyard. About 30 minutes inside.

  3. 3
    10:15

    Down the Talaa Kebira

    The main lane runs downhill past bakeries, butchers, spice stalls and old fountains. Your guide explains how the medina still works, street by street.

  4. 4
    10:45

    Nejjarine and Al-Attarine

    The Nejjarine fountain and its woodwork museum in an old caravanserai, then the Al-Attarine Madrasa and a look through the doors of the Qaraouiyine (Muslims only inside).

  5. 5
    11:45

    Seffarine and the Chouara Tannery

    Coppersmiths at work on Seffarine Square, then the dye pits from a leather shop terrace. Mint leaves help with the smell; you never have to buy.

  6. 6
    12:30

    Rooftop tea and the way back

    Mint tea on a rooftop café, then your guide walks you to your riad or to a taxi at Bab Rcif, or shows you how to explore alone.

Good to know

  • Lanes are steep in places and slippery when wet: wear closed shoes
  • Donkeys and carts pass in the lanes: step aside when you hear “balak”
  • On Friday many shops close, so the souks are quieter
  • Wheelchair or stroller? Tell us: the medina has steps; we plan the best way

Costs on the day

  • Bou Inania Madrasa: about 20 MAD per adult
  • Al-Attarine Madrasa: about 20 MAD per adult
  • Nejjarine Museum: about 20 MAD per adult (optional)
  • Lunch: around €10 depending on what you eat
  • Tips: up to you
  • Cash: bring dirhams; ticket desks and small shops prefer cash

Frequently Asked Questions

Will we be taken to shops?

No, not unless you ask. Fes is known for tours that end in carpet and leather shops; ours earns nothing from them. To see the tannery we use a leather shop terrace, and you leave without buying.

Is a guide worth it in the Fes medina?

Yes, more than in any other Moroccan city. The lanes are a real maze, phone maps lose their way, and young men offer to “show you the tannery” for money. With your guide nobody bothers you, and he shows you landmarks to find your way alone.

How hard is the walk?

About 2 km in 3 to 4 hours, mostly downhill from Bab Boujeloud. Some lanes are steep and stepped. Wear closed shoes; we stop at cafés whenever you want.

Can we go inside the Qaraouiyine?

The mosque is open to Muslims only. Everyone can look into its courtyard from the doors, and the Al-Attarine Madrasa next door shows the same art inside.

What should we wear?

Comfortable closed shoes, and clothes that cover shoulders and knees, out of respect. A light scarf helps in the sun and at the tannery.
