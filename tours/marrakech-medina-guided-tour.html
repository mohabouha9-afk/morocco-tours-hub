Overview

Walk the old city of Marrakech with a local guide: the Koutoubia minaret, the painted rooms of the Bahia Palace, the Mellah, Jemaa el-Fnaa and the souks, ending at the Ben Youssef Madrasa.

It is your private tour, so you set the pace, choose the stops and browse as long as you like. Do it on your first day: you will find your own way through the lanes afterwards.

Highlights

  • Koutoubia Mosque: the 12th-century minaret, seen from its gardens
  • Bahia Palace: painted cedar ceilings, zellige tiles and marble courtyards
  • The Mellah: the Lazama Synagogue, the covered spice market and Place des Ferblantiers, the square of the lantern makers
  • Jemaa el-Fnaa: the great square at the heart of the medina
  • The souks: Souk Semmarine, Rahba Kedima spice square and the dyers’ souk
  • Ben Youssef Madrasa: the carved cedar and stucco courtyard of the 16th-century Koranic school

Programme

  1. 1
    09:00

    Meet your guide

    Your guide meets you at your riad door or the nearest gate; from a hotel outside the medina, your driver brings you to the Koutoubia. We confirm the time on WhatsApp the day before.

  2. 2
    09:15

    Koutoubia and the Kasbah lanes

    Start under the 12th-century minaret, seen from its gardens (the mosque is open to Muslims only), and hear how the Red City grew around it.

  3. 3
    09:45

    Bahia Palace

    About 45 minutes in painted cedar rooms and tiled courtyards, before the tour groups arrive around 11:00. Entry is paid at the gate.

  4. 4
    10:45

    The Mellah

    Walk the old Jewish quarter, its covered spice market and the square of the lantern makers, with a stop at a synagogue if you wish.

  5. 5
    11:30

    Jemaa el-Fnaa and the souks

    Cross the great square into the souks: spice square, dyers, leather and metal workers. Free time to browse; we enter a shop only if you ask.

  6. 6
    12:30

    Ben Youssef Madrasa and rooftop tea

    The carved courtyard of the old Koranic school, then mint tea on a rooftop café. Your guide walks you back, or shows you the way to explore on your own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will we be taken to shops?

No, not unless you ask. Many medina tours earn commissions from shops; ours does not. If you want to buy, your guide helps you find fair prices, and you never have to buy anything.

Is a guide worth it in the medina?

On your first day, yes. The lanes are a maze, signposts are few and people offer to “help” for a fee. With your guide nobody bothers you, and he shows you how to find your way back alone.

How much walking is it, and is it hot?

About 5 km on flat lanes, with stops all the way. In summer we start early, or at 15:00 when the sun is lower. Bring water and a hat; we can slow down or cut a stop at any time.

What should we wear?

Comfortable closed shoes for the uneven lanes, and clothes that cover shoulders and knees, out of respect. A light scarf is useful in the sun.

Can we see the tanneries?

Yes, if you ask. Strangers often lead visitors to the tanneries and then ask for money; with your guide you go straight there, see the dye pits from a terrace and leave when you like.
