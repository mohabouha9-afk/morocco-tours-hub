Overview

Eat your way through the medina as the evening starts: olives and spices in the Mellah, msemen and harira where locals stop after work, slow-roasted mechoui, then the night stalls of Jemaa el-Fnaa.

It is your private tour, so your guide picks each stall, agrees the price before you sit down and keeps the pace yours. About ten tastings, all included, are enough for dinner.

Highlights

  • Mellah spice market: cured olives, dates, preserved lemon and the spices of ras el hanout
  • Riad Zitoun el Kedim: msemen off the griddle with honey and amlou, and harira soup
  • Mechoui alley: lamb from the underground ovens and tanjia from the hammam furnace
  • Jemaa el-Fnaa: snail broth, fresh orange juice and the night stalls
  • Pastries and mint tea: chebakia and gazelle horns on a terrace above the square

Programme

  1. 1
    17:30

    Meet your food guide

    At your riad door, or at the Koutoubia if your driver brings you from a hotel outside the medina. We confirm the time on WhatsApp the day before.

  2. 2
    17:45

    Mellah spice market

    Taste cured olives, dates and preserved lemon among the stalls of the old Jewish quarter, and learn what goes into ras el hanout.

  3. 3
    18:30

    Msemen and harira

    On Riad Zitoun el Kedim, the street where locals stop after work: flaky msemen off the griddle with honey and amlou, then a bowl of harira soup.

  4. 4
    19:15

    Mechoui alley

    Lamb slow-roasted in underground ovens, sliced by weight with cumin and salt, and tanjia, the Marrakech stew cooked overnight in the hammam furnace.

  5. 5
    20:15

    Jemaa el-Fnaa at night

    Snail broth for the brave and a fresh orange juice, among the musicians and storytellers. Your guide picks each stall and agrees the price first.

  6. 6
    21:00

    Pastries, mint tea and back

    Chebakia and gazelle horns with mint tea on a terrace above the square, then your guide walks you home or your driver takes you back by about 21:30.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the street food safe?

Yes, with the right stalls. The stalls of the square are inspected by the city, and we only take you to busy ones we know, where the food is cooked in front of you and sold fast. If your stomach is sensitive, we skip raw salads and tap water.

What about the touts at the square’s stalls?

Some stalls in the middle of the square pull you in, bring bread and olives you did not order and charge for everything. With your guide you sit where locals sit, and the price is agreed before anything arrives.

How much food is it?

About ten tastings, which add up to a full dinner. Come hungry: skip lunch or eat lightly, and say when you have had enough.

Vegetarian, vegan or allergies?

Yes. Without the mechoui there is still plenty: msemen, harira, olives, bissara, juice and pastries. Tell us about allergies when you book. All the meat is halal and there is no pork anywhere.

Can children come?

Yes. Children love the msemen, the juice and the pastries, and the snails are only for those who want them. We keep the pace easy and can finish earlier.
