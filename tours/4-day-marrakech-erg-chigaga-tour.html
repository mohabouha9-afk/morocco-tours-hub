Overview

Four days from Marrakech to Erg Chigaga, the largest sand sea in Morocco and the hardest to reach. You cross the High Atlas and follow the palm groves of the Draa Valley to Zagora. At M’Hamid, where the road ends, your 4×4 leaves the asphalt for the last 50 km of desert track to the dunes: a camel ride at sunset, dinner in the camp and a sky with no lights for miles.

You come back a different way, across the Iriqui dry lake on the old Paris–Dakar track, then a night in Ouarzazate and a relaxed last morning at Aït Ben Haddou. Because the tour is private, you stop when you want, eat where you like and never stop at a shop you didn’t ask for.

Itinerary

  1. 1
    Day 1

    Marrakech → Tizi n’Tichka → Ouarzazate → Draa Valley → Zagora

    Pick-up at 07:00 at your hotel, or at the nearest gate if your riad is in the medina. The road climbs over the High Atlas by the Tizi n’Tichka pass (2,260 m), with photo stops, down to Ouarzazate for lunch. Then over the Tizi n’Tinifift pass to Agdz and down the Draa Valley, mile after mile of date palms and earthen kasbahs, to Zagora for dinner.

    • About 360 km, about 7 hours on the road
    • Night: Zagora
    • Dinner & breakfast
  2. 2
    Day 2

    Zagora → Tamegroute → M’Hamid → Erg Chigaga

    After breakfast, Tamegroute: its green-glazed pottery workshops and a library of old Koranic manuscripts. Lunch in M’Hamid, the last village, where the road ends. Your 4×4 then crosses stony plains, dry riverbeds and soft sand to the dunes of Erg Chigaga. A camel ride over the dunes at sunset, dinner in the camp and music around the fire under a sky with no lights for miles.

    • About 100 km by road, then 50 km of desert track
    • Camel ride about 1 hour
    • Night: Erg Chigaga camp
    • Breakfast & dinner
  3. 3
    Day 3

    Erg Chigaga → Iriqui dry lake → Foum Zguid → Ouarzazate

    Up early for sunrise on the dunes and a go on the sandboards before breakfast. Then west by 4×4 across the Iriqui dry lake, flat to the horizon, on the old Paris–Dakar rally track to Foum Zguid, where the road begins again. Lunch, then through Taznakht, the carpet-weaving town, to Ouarzazate for dinner.

    • About 100 km of desert track, then 155 km by road
    • Night: Ouarzazate
    • Breakfast & dinner
  4. 4
    Day 4

    Ouarzazate → Aït Ben Haddou → Tizi n’Tichka → Marrakech

    A short drive to Aït Ben Haddou in the soft morning light. Your driver-guide walks you up through the ksar to the old granary at the top, with views across the valley. Lunch nearby, then back over the High Atlas to Marrakech; you are at your hotel around 17:00.

    • About 205 km, about 4 hours on the road
    • In Marrakech around 17:00
    • Breakfast

Frequently Asked Questions

Erg Chigaga or Merzouga: which should we choose?

Merzouga has the tallest dunes, up to about 150 m, and a paved road to the edge of the sand, but also many camps. The dunes of Erg Chigaga are lower, about 50–60 m, yet the sand sea is bigger and almost empty: few camps, no tour buses, real silence. Choose Chigaga for remoteness, Merzouga for the classic giant dunes.

What is the off-road drive like?

From M’Hamid it is about 50 km of desert track, 1.5 to 2.5 hours, over stony plains, dry riverbeds and soft sand; the way out across the Iriqui lake is longer. It is bumpy in places but safe in a 4×4 with a driver who knows the tracks, and you stop whenever you want.

Do the camps have private bathrooms?

The standard camp in our table has shared bathrooms. The midrange and luxury camps have a private bathroom in your tent, with a flushing toilet and a shower. Water and power come by 4×4 this far out, so camps are simpler than in Merzouga at the same level.

Can we ride a camel at sunrise too?

Yes. The sunset ride, about 1 hour, is included. For a second ride at sunrise, tell us when you book or ask at the camp the evening before. If you would rather not ride at all, your 4×4 takes you straight to the camp.

When is the best time to go?

October to April is the most comfortable. From December to February nights drop close to freezing, so bring a warm layer; the camps have heavy blankets. June to August days pass 40 °C, and spring can bring sand winds. In winter, snow sometimes closes the Tichka pass for a few hours.
